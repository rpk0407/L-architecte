# Le lexique : la voix du salon

How Barber L’Architecte talks, so that every sentence a designer writes or reviews sounds like it. The examples in French are the product’s own. The notes are in English.

## The register

**Tu, always.** The shop says *tu* to everyone: warm, direct, Marseille. It is the identity and it is not up for editing by a designer; see tension [T-03](03-tensions.md). The discipline it asks of the writer: **the sentences that carry a rule** (a deposit, a cancellation, a membership, an erasure) are written in a calm, plain register that works for a 22-year-old and for a 67-year-old. No slang, no joke, no pressure.

**Short.** One idea per sentence. A noun phrase can be a sentence: « Les prix, sans surprise. » « Sorti du fauteuil. »

**Concrete.** A day, an hour, a name, an amount: « Mercredi 28 octobre · 9 h · avec Frank · 20 € ». Never « bientôt », « rapidement », « selon disponibilités ».

**Gender-neutral by construction.** *Tu* is neutral; address a person without a gendered form (« Bon retour », not « Bienvenue, cher client »). The clientele is mostly men and is not only men: mothers book for sons, women book fades, a gift-giver is anyone.

**The wordplay.** *Coupe* is a haircut and an architect’s section drawing; the shop plays on both (« Sorti du fauteuil », « On recoupe ? », « Le temps passe sur ta coupe »). Use it where it costs nothing and never where it could confuse (an error message, a rule).

## Words

| Say | Not | Why |
|---|---|---|
| un rendez-vous, une place | une prestation (in the client’s screens) | « prestation » is the shop’s word, not the client’s |
| une coupe, un dégradé, une barbe | un service capillaire | the words clients use |
| Sans préférence | N’importe quel barbier | the first respects the choice, the second dismisses it |
| un acompte | un dépôt de garantie, une pénalité | « acompte » says it is deducted; « pénalité » says he is guilty |
| créneau | (fine) | everyone understands it; « une place » is warmer in speech |
| Itinéraire | Comment s’y rendre | shorter, and it is what the button does |
| Déplacer, Annuler | Modifier votre réservation | the two verbs he came for |
| facultatif | optionnel | the shop’s own word on the form |
| résilier | mettre fin à votre engagement | the word on the law and in his head |

**Hair-talk.** The stranger has a photo and no vocabulary. A *dégradé* is a cut that is short at the sides and rises gradually; a *fondu* is the same, blended to the skin; a *contour* is the edging of the beard line; a *taille* is trimming and shaping; a *sabot* is a clipper guard. Explain on first use, once, in the service’s own line (« Coupe + contour de barbe · Coupe · Contour de la barbe »); never in a glossary he has to find. Do not guess clipper numbers: they differ between manufacturers.

## The shapes of a good sentence

**A button** says what it will do: « Réserver et régler l’acompte », not « Confirmer » when money is next. « Reprendre » for « Comme la dernière fois ? ».

**A rule** is said once, with its reason, before the button: *amount, reason, what if he cancels*. « Un acompte de 15 € est demandé pour cette prestation, déduit au salon. Annulé plus de 12 h avant, il est remboursé. »

**An error** says what happened, what is safe, and what to do, and never blames. « Pas de connexion. Vérifie ton réseau et réessaie. » « Ce créneau vient d’être pris. Choisis-en un autre, tes choix sont gardés. » (The second is perfect, when it is true.)

**An empty state** says what will be here and how it gets here. **A confirmation** says it is done and names everything: « C’est réservé, Julien. »

**Why we ask.** Every field says why: « Pour la confirmation et le rappel, rien d’autre. »

## Typography in French

- Prices: **20 €**, with a no-break space before the euro sign (and **15 €** in the button, not « 15€ »). Decimals with a comma: **1 234,50 €**.
- Times: **9 h 30**, **18 h 30**, with no-break spaces; the 24-hour clock.
- Dates: **mardi 27 octobre**, no capital on the day or the month; **mar. 27 oct.** when short.
- Apostrophes and quotes: ’ and « … » with no-break spaces inside.
- **13<sup>e</sup>** for the arrondissement.
- Punctuation that wants a space before it in French (: ; ? !) gets a **no-break** one, so it never starts a line.

## The read-aloud test

Read the sentence aloud, in the shop, to the person it is for. If you would not say it across the counter to a man of sixty or a boy of seventeen, rewrite it.
